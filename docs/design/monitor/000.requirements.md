# Monitoring and Observability Requirements

Status: target observability requirements; current runtime instrumentation is partial.

## Scope and rationale

Provide metrics, traces, and logs that reduce incident detection/recovery time,
protect availability and latency SLOs, and support capacity and product planning.
The [system architecture](../../architecture.md) and
[boundary decision](../../../.agents/notes/proposed/architecture/2026-10-10-platform-console-instance-boundaries.md)
assign fleet operations to employee Management, owned-instance views to the
separate developer Console, and application services to instances.

Employee, developer, and application identities have distinct authority.
Employee fleet visibility does not make an application token a platform token;
a developer sees authorized instances/projects/databases. Telemetry access is
separately authorized and audited, including any employee access to scoped data.
The current embedded console and partial runtime metrics do not supply these
cross-product monitoring or identity boundaries.

## Goals and scope

Public requests and background jobs require joinable telemetry. Define SLOs for
REST, replication, triggers, realtime, and instance Identity, with clear paging
owners and runbooks. Dashboards cover fleet, instance, project, and database
slices as appropriate. Pipelines tolerate backpressure and backend outages
without blocking business traffic.

Use OpenTelemetry and supported exporters, starting with Go services and the
TypeScript SDK. Customer-managed observability stacks remain integration choices;
this design does not replace them or introduce a custom tracing protocol.

## Correlation and isolation

Every signal identifies its emitting service, deployment environment, region,
and version. Attach instance identity for instance operations, project identity
for project/realm operations, and database identity for database operations.
Employee fleet scheduling or a developer account request can have no database
scope. Collectors validate the applicable scope; they must not invent identifiers
or reject correctly scoped platform signals for lacking a database.

Request, trace, and span identifiers correlate individual work in logs/traces.
Principal identifiers are permitted only under privacy policy and must indicate
the actor's authority; they are not metric labels. Per-instance/project/database
volume and sampling budgets protect the pipeline from noisy neighbors.

## Signal requirements

- Metrics capture RED for HTTP/gRPC/realtime endpoints and USE for CPU, memory,
  goroutines, database pools, queues, and other critical resources. Domain metrics
  include replication lag, trigger dispatch, query planning, storage latency,
  and Identity authentication outcomes. Labels use bounded service/route/status
  dimensions and authorized instance/project/database slices.
- Traces propagate W3C context across HTTP, gRPC, realtime, SDK, and outbound
  dependencies. Include request and query-plan correlation when applicable.
  Baseline sampling targets 10% healthy traffic; errors and operations exceeding
  their latency target receive 100% sampling subject to bounded capacity.
- Logs are structured JSON with timestamp, severity, service, message, and
  applicable request/trace/scope fields. Redact credentials and tokens; hash
  sensitive identifiers unless raw values are required and authorized. INFO
  covers lifecycle, WARN recoverable conditions, and ERROR user-impacting failure;
  noisy categories use sampling.

## SLOs, alerting, and operations

Targets include API availability 99.9%, realtime availability 99.5%, per-operation
latency, replication freshness, and trigger success. Multi-window burn-rate
policies page on fast budget loss and warn on slow loss. Alerts contain a runbook,
owning team, recent deployment, and relevant error/scope/region context.

Service dashboards show golden signals, saturation, dependencies, and deployment
markers. Management shows fleet capacity and operational rollups; Console shows
only authorized developer instances and their relevant project/database views.
Rollups include incident counts, uptime, MTTR/MTTD, and telemetry cost. Post-incident
reviews feed instrumentation, sampling, and alert changes back into the design.

## Retention and engineering limits

Default targets: metrics 30–90 days with 15 days of high resolution, traces 7–14
days with error indexing up to 30 days, and logs 14–30 days plus cold archive.
Sampling is configurable by environment and authorized resource tier.

Instrumentation targets less than 5% CPU and 10% added critical-path latency.
Telemetry export must fail open for business traffic; drop/retry counters expose
loss. Collector/backend ingestion availability targets 99.9%. TLS, authenticated
publishers, dashboard authorization, redaction/schema checks, and audited data
access are required.

## Integrations and unresolved choices

The target stack uses OTel collectors, Prometheus, Tempo/Jaeger, Loki or
ClickHouse, Alertmanager, and Grafana with recording rules and trace exemplars.
The logs backend and archive strategy remain open based on measured cost and
retention. Developer-visible custom SLOs and indexed query-plan/scope attributes
require product and cardinality decisions. Current deployment dashboards are
local tooling, not delivered employee Management or developer Console features.
